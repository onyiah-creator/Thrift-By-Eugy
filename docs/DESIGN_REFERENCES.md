# Thrift by Eugy — what was taken from the reference sites, and what wasn't

## From Fashion Nova

| Pattern | How it's used here |
|---|---|
| **"Trend Report" — named editorial collections** | Rebuilt as **The Edit**: Owambe Ready, The Lagos Workweek, Denim Broken In, Quiet Luxury |
| Tabbed product rail ("Shop the Latest") | For You / New In / Dresses / Under ₦10k |
| Recently viewed as a nav-level feature | Auto-populates as items are opened |
| Wishlist | ♥ save, with a note that saving doesn't reserve the item |
| Lifestyle category tiles | Category strip + circular entries |
| Prominent size guide | Nav-level, plus a link inside every product view |

**The editorial edit is the single most valuable idea here.** Fashion Nova
uses named collections to package trends. You can use them to package *taste* —
which is the actual product a thrift store sells. You are not competing on
price or breadth; you're competing on having a good eye, and named edits are
how you make that visible and shoppable.

The edit names are deliberately local (Owambe, Lagos Workweek). Generic trend
language is the one thing you can't win at — a Lagos-specific point of view is.

## From Shein

| **Price-forward product cards** | Price is the largest element on the card, above the name |
| Dense mobile-first grid | 2 columns on mobile, 4 on desktop |
| Persistent terms bar above the header | Carries scarcity + delivery + returns |

## From Pinterest

Masonry discovery feed, save-to-collection, and hover reveal on cards.

## Deliberately NOT copied

**Countdown timers.** Fashion Nova runs a live ticking timer in the top bar.
It works for them because their stock is effectively infinite, so urgency has
to be manufactured. Yours is genuinely one-of-one. Real scarcity stated plainly
("Only one available") is more credible than a timer, and a fake timer on a
genuinely scarce item actively cheapens it.

**Percentage-off badges.** Both sites are covered in 55% / 70% / 80% OFF.
A one-of-one preloved piece has no "original price" to discount from —
inventing one is misleading and undercuts the curation story. Value here is
communicated through the piece and the price, not a struck-through number.

**Category sprawl.** Shein spans 26 categories from clothing to automotive.
Six focused categories signals curation. Breadth signals liquidation.

**"Trend-first" positioning.** Both sites sell newness and disposability.
Thrift's appeal is the opposite — longevity, character, and things that were
made better than they are now. The hero copy leans into that: *"Someone
already loved this first."*

## Thrift-specific additions neither site needs

- **"1 available" on every card.** Sets expectations before the click.
- **Saving ≠ reserving.** Stated explicitly in the saved panel. On one-of-one
  stock a shopper who assumes a save holds the item will be angry when it sells.
- **Condition grade on every card.** The single most important trust signal in
  secondhand, and the thing shoppers look for first.
- **Size guide framed around vintage inconsistency.** A vintage "L" is often a
  modern "M". Telling people to compare flat measurements to a garment they
  already own prevents the return that would otherwise happen.
